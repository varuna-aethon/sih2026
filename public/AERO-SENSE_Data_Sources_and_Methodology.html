SECTION 01

System Overview

Modern numerical weather prediction (NWP) models developed by premier meteorological centers produce dependable continental and regional synoptic outlooks. However, operational global and regional ensemble suites—including the NCMRWF Ensemble Prediction System (NEPS-G), ECMWF IFS, and NCEP GFS—operate at grid cell resolutions of 12 km to 25 km. At this spatial scale, localized convective cloudbursts, severe urban heat micro-islands, inner-core cyclonic eye wind shears, and steep mountain orographic rain rates are significantly dampened by mathematical grid-diffusion.

VARUNA was designed to address Smart India Hackathon 2026 Problem Statement SIH26078, sponsored by the Ministry of Earth Sciences (MoES) and the National Centre for Medium Range Weather Forecasting (NCMRWF). Its mission is to bridge the operational gap between coarse NWP synoptic envelopes and actionable, hyper-local disaster governance.

By uniting continuous multi-source surface telemetry, dual-API cross-verification, physical climatological baseline distributions, 4D spatio-temporal track reconstruction, synchronized NASA satellite imagery, and generative downscaling physics, VARUNA refines broad synoptic hazard perimeters into a calibrated 5 km circular impact zone (78.5 km² vs 3,500 km² district revenue warnings). It automatically compiles institutional Common Alerting Protocol (CAP-IN v1.0) payloads and multi-lingual citizen advisories for immediate emergency dispatch.

SECTION 05

Scientific Method Notes

1. Extreme Forecast Index (EFI) Formulation

The EFI measures anomalous weather severity by calculating the integrated difference between the cumulative distribution function (CDF) of the ensemble forecast F_f(x) and the model’s 30-year historical climatology F_c(x):

EFI = (2 / π) · ∫ [ (F_c(x) - F_f(x)) / √(F_c(x) · (1 - F_c(x))) ] dx

The denominator weights the tails where rare catastrophic extremes reside. When EFI z-score reaches +4.35σ with 30/30 member convergence, AERO-SENSE triggers a CATASTROPHIC alert classification.

2. False-Alarm Area Reduction Calculation

Conventional disaster advisories blanket whole administrative revenue districts (~3,500 km² for Purba Medinipur). AERO-SENSE downscales the physical strike trajectory to a calibrated 5 km circular radius (r = 5 km):

A_pinpoint = π · r² = 3.14159 · 25 = 78.54 km²
Reduction = [ (3,500 - 78.54) / 3,500 ] × 100% = 97.76% ≈ 97.8%

This represents a 44.6× spatial precision gain, shielding over 3.68 million non-vulnerable citizens from unnecessary economic shutdown while concentrating evacuation transport along the strike sector.

3. Historical Analog Matching Vector Metric

The Historical Analog Matching Engine evaluates similarity between an active storm profile and candidate historical events across a 30-year database using a weighted vector distance:

S_total = 0.35 · S_spatial + 0.30 · S_intensity + 0.20 · S_pressure + 0.15 · S_intensification

Where S_spatial evaluates track distance (1 - Δd / 500 km), S_intensity evaluates sustained wind delta (1 - |ΔV| / 50 kt), S_pressure evaluates central deficit, and S_intensification evaluates 24h pressure deepening.

4. Real-Time Automatic Anomaly Detection Loop

Every 60 seconds, each station's current observations are compared against its 10-year historical same-hour climatological baseline distribution (μ, σ) to calculate real-time z-scores:

z = ( x_current - μ_climatology ) / σ_climatology

Any station where |z| ≥ 2.0σ on temperature, wind velocity, or precipitation is immediately promoted into the active Anomaly Watch list with an auto-synthesized mission status and severity alert banner.
